# Threads drafts for review

These are draft pairs, not queued posts. Pick the version assigned to the intended account after reviewing the wording and destination. No publication time, new cadence, approval, spend, or tracking change is proposed here.

The audience is a person building a useful product with AI and following a live build. Each parent offers a concrete action. The separate reply carries the invitation, matching the existing Threads workflow. The proof notes describe what to demonstrate, not an event claimed to have happened. Use a disposable example project in any recording.

The claims about the shared canvas and agent cards follow the repository's [messaging brief](../../colateral-messaging.md). There are no revenue, customer, launch, savings, price, or pack-availability claims.

## 1. Finish one task

**Hook:** A demo has to work beyond its first screen.

**Short parent:**

Your AI can build a screen. Can someone finish the job it was built for? Try one complete task before adding another feature.

**Threads parent:**

Start with a person opening your app for the first time. Give them one job, then watch them finish it without hints. The spot where they stop is a more useful next build than another feature on your list.

**Proof to show:** A disposable app input, its saved result, and a fresh reopen. Keep a failed step visible if the task stops there.

## 7. Make failure part of the tool

**Hook:** A real workflow needs a recovery step.

**Short parent:**

**Threads parent:**

Build the unhappy path while the app is still small. Remove a test file, interrupt a save, and check the message someone sees. Can they recover their work and try again without guessing which step actually finished?

**Proof to show:** An intentionally failed save against synthetic data and its recovery. Do not imply the current live app passed this check without a receipt.

## 8. Turn expertise into a small tool

**Hook:** Start with a job you can judge.

**Short parent:**

Pick a job you know well before asking AI to build a business around it. You can spot the missing step that a polished demo hides.

**Threads parent:**

Choose one recurring task you understand. Make a small tool for that step, try it on a disposable example, and compare the result with doing it yourself. The useful question is whether it handles the job clearly, including mistakes.

**Proof to show:** A tool idea such as sorting a sample file, with expected output and an invalid input. This is a build exercise, not a claim that a bundled tool exists.
